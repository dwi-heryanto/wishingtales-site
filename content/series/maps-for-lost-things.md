## Premise

On Tuesdays and Saturdays, Juno helps her grandmother in a shop that has no sign, only a brass bell, and a window full of rolled paper. People come in to ask for things they have lost: a key, a dog, a word, a way of speaking to a brother. Juno draws them a map — and the map is right, though never in the way the customer expected. Each part is one customer, one map, one walk. The series is about what people actually lose, which is usually not the thing they asked for.

## The place

The shop is on Corvid Lane, which is real enough to have a bakery and a bus stop, and odd enough that the lane's far end is never the same twice. The maps are drawn by hand on brown paper with three pencils: black for what is there, red for what is not, blue for what is true. Juno is not allowed to use the blue pencil yet.

## Characters

- **Juno**, twelve, the mapmaker's apprentice. Observant, literal-minded, secretly competitive. She wants to be good at this before she has earned it.
- **Grandmother (Oda)**, patient, dry, never answers the question that was asked. She is not mysterious for the sake of it; she is old and busy and has rules for good reasons.
- **The customers**, one per part, taken seriously. A boy who has lost his nerve before a race. A woman who has lost the recipe her mother used. A dog walker who has lost the sound of a name.

## Tone

Cosy, precise, slightly strange. The wonder is in the drawing, not in the shouting. Nothing is dangerous; the tension is whether Juno will draw the honest map or the kind one.

## How a part works

1. A customer arrives with a loss — and states it wrongly, in a way that is almost true.
2. Juno and Oda disagree about what the map should show.
3. Juno draws it. Describe the drawing closely; this is the best image in each part.
4. Juno walks the map with the customer, or follows it alone when the customer cannot.
5. What was lost is found, but not the thing that was asked for — and the customer is glad anyway. End on the empty shop and the pencils going back in the tin.

## Continuity rules

- One customer, one part. Never carry a customer across parts.
- The blue pencil stays in its drawer until the last part of the series.
- Oda never explains the rules of the shop. She only applies them.
- The maps are ordinary objects: paper, pencil, fold lines, a smudge.
