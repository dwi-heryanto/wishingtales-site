## Premise

Nell and Tessa Avery live with their father in the lamp room above Garrow Harbour, where the light has to be lit every evening and kept burning until dawn. Their mother is away for the winter, working on the mainland. Each part is one night: something goes wrong, one of the sisters has to deal with it, and the light has to still be burning when the sun comes up. The series is about two children being trusted with something that matters, and slowly learning how to trust each other.

## The place

Garrow is a fishing harbour at the end of a long road: thirty houses, a stone pier, a shop that opens when it feels like it, and the tower on the headland. The weather is a character. The sea is never described as beautiful; it is described as work.

## Characters

- **Nell**, eleven, the younger sister. Careful, good with her hands, keeps a tally of things in a notebook. She is brave in the way of someone who is frightened and goes anyway.
- **Tessa**, thirteen, the elder. Quick, funny, restless, allergic to being told what to do. She resents the tower and would never say so.
- **Their father, Iwan Avery**, the keeper. Tired, kind, says less than he means. He is not absent — he is asleep, or out on the pier, or called away by a boat.
- **Mr Caddy**, the harbour master, who appears in two or three parts at most, mostly to be wrong about something.

## Tone

Quiet, salt-air, practical. Warmth comes from the sisters' talk, not from the narrator. The scary parts are weather and darkness, never monsters. Nobody dies. Nothing is explained with magic — everything that happens could happen.

## How a part works

1. Open inside the evening routine: lighting the lamp, supper, the last boat in.
2. Something is wrong — a broken part, a missing boat, a storm turning, a stranger on the pier.
3. The sisters disagree about what to do, briefly and believably.
4. One of them acts; the other has to decide whether to follow.
5. The night resolves, the light is still burning, and one small thing has changed between them. End on an ordinary image: a kettle, wet boots, the lamp going out at dawn.

## Continuity rules

- Each part stands alone. A reader who starts at part four should be fine.
- Never resolve the sisters' central disagreement; let it move a little each time.
- Keep the mother offstage and mentioned rarely — a card at Christmas, a parcel, a phone call that costs money.
- No cliffhangers. The promise is that the light stays on.
