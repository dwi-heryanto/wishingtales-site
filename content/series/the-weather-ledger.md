## Premise

The village of Highside keeps a ledger — a hundred years of evenings, one line each: wind, rain, frost, sun. Otis, whose grandfather kept it before him, writes the entry every night by the window. Then one night there is already a line written, in a hand that is not his. The series is about a boy being answered by something enormous and patient, and deciding what to ask it.

## The place

Highside is seven houses, a chapel, a phone box, and a hill that the weather arrives over. The village is small enough that everyone knows the ledger exists and nobody reads it. The hill is the highest point for miles; the sky is the closest thing to a character the series has.

## Characters

- **Otis**, twelve, keeps the ledger because his grandfather cannot any more. Methodical, literal, keeps his worry in his pockets. Says "right" when he means yes.
- **Grandfather (Bram)**, in the next room, mostly asleep, occasionally luminous. His memory goes; his opinions do not.
- **Mira**, who delivers the post on a bicycle and believes nothing until she has seen it. She is not a sidekick — she argues.
- **The ledger hand**, patient, formal, oddly old-fashioned. It answers exactly what it is asked, and nothing more.

## Tone

Slow, bright, matter-of-fact. This is the gentlest series of the three: the wonder is quiet and the stakes are personal rather than physical. Nothing chases anybody.

## How a part works

1. Evening routine, and the entry for the day — describe the weather as work, not decoration.
2. A new line appears, or an answer to what was asked last time.
3. Otis tests it, carefully, with an experiment rather than a demand.
4. What he learns is true but not useful in the way he wanted.
5. He writes the evening's entry anyway, honestly. End on the pen, the page, the lamp going off, the hill outside.

## Continuity rules

- The weather never speaks aloud. Everything arrives as handwriting.
- One question per part, and never more than one answer.
- Mira stays sceptical for at least four parts.
- Grandfather's decline is handled with dignity, never as a plot device or a scare. He does not die in this series.
