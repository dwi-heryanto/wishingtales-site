The heat came in waves off the sidewalk, even after the sun went down. Maya sat on the porch steps with her little brother Leo, both of them holding glasses of lemonade that had gone warm an hour ago.

"It's too hot to move," Leo said. He flopped back against the step and let his arms hang.

"Too hot to even think," Maya said. She fanned herself with a paper plate.

Inside, the air conditioner hummed and hummed. Then it stopped. The hum cut off like a held breath. The kitchen light flickered and died. The whole house went dark and quiet.

Leo sat up fast. "Maya?" His voice was small.

"I'm right here," she said.

"Why did the lights go out?"

"Power's out," Maya said. She stood and felt her way to the screen door. "Probably the heat. Everybody's running their AC at once."

Leo scrambled up and grabbed her arm. His hand was sweaty. "Is it going to stay out?"

"Not forever," Maya said. "Come on. Let's find a flashlight."

Inside, the dark pressed close. The hallway was a long black tunnel. Leo's breath came fast and shallow.

"I don't like it," he whispered.

"I know," Maya said. "But we're okay. The dark can't hurt you."

"It feels like it can."

Maya squeezed his hand. "Then we'll make some light. That's all. Just a little light."

***

Maya knew the kitchen drawer by heart: the one beside the stove, full of rubber bands and old takeout menus. She pulled it open and felt around. Her fingers found a flashlight. She clicked the button. Nothing.

"Dead," she said.

"Try another one," Leo said. He was pressed against her side, his voice right by her elbow.

She found a second flashlight in the junk drawer. Click. Nothing. A third one under the sink. Click. Nothing.

"All dead," Maya said. She put them on the counter.

"All of them?" Leo's voice wobbled.

"Batteries," Maya said. "They've been sitting too long."

"Can we get new ones?"

"The store's probably closed," Maya said. "And the car's in the shop."

Leo was quiet. Then he said, "It's so dark." His hand found hers again and held tight. "It's darker than outside."

Maya looked toward the window. Outside, the sky was a deep blue, not quite black. The moon was up. She could see the shape of the big oak tree.

"Come on," she said. "Let's check the living room. Maybe there's a candle."

They walked slowly. Leo bumped into the coffee table and yelped. Maya steadied him. "You're okay," she said.

"I'm not," Leo said. "I can't see anything."

Maya felt along the mantel. Her fingers found a candle, half-melted and dusty. But no matches. She kept looking. In the hallway closet, she found a box of old birthday candles. Still no matches.

"Nothing," she said.

Leo sat down on the floor and pulled his knees up. "We're going to be in the dark all night," he said. His voice cracked.

Maya sat beside him. The floor was warm. The house was too quiet. She could hear her own heartbeat.

"I don't know what to do," she said. She said it out loud, because it was true.

Leo leaned against her. "I want the lights back," he whispered.

Maya put her arm around him. Outside, the dark was not so dark. Something blinked near the bushes. Then blinked again.

Maya sat up straight. "Leo," she said. "Remember the fireflies?" Leo lifted his head. "What fireflies?" "Last summer," Maya said. "In the backyard. They blinked like little stars." Leo wiped his face. "I remember," he said. "They were everywhere." Maya stood up and pulled him to his feet. "We can catch them," she said. "In a jar. Then we'll have light." Leo's eyes went wide. "Really?" "Really," Maya said. She felt her way to the kitchen. In the cupboard under the sink, she found an empty jar. It had a lid with holes punched in it. She grabbed it. "Perfect," she said. Leo followed her to the back door. The door creaked open. Warm air rushed in. The grass was cool and soft under their feet. Crickets sang. Maya held the jar. "Look for blinking," she said. Leo pointed. "There!" A tiny light flickered near the fence. Then another. And another. The backyard sparkled. Maya crept toward the fence. She held the jar open. A firefly drifted inside. She clapped the lid on. "Got one!" The jar glowed. Leo laughed. "It's like a tiny lantern," he said. Maya smiled. "Let's get more," she said.

***

They caught fireflies one by one. Maya would spot a blink, then Leo would cup his hands and guide it toward the jar. "Easy," Maya said. "Don't squash it." "I'm being careful," Leo said. He caught two in his palms. He peeked between his fingers. "It tickles," he said. Maya laughed. "Put it in." Leo opened his hands over the jar. The firefly flew in. Soon the jar held seven or eight. It glowed like a small moon. Leo held it up. "We did it!" he said. The light made his face soft and gold. Maya felt proud. "Now we have a night light," she said. They started back toward the house. The grass was wet with dew. Leo took a big step and tripped on a root. The jar flew out of his hands. It hit the ground with a thud. The lid popped off. Fireflies spilled out. They blinked once, twice, and then rose into the air. Maya gasped. "No!" She dropped to her knees. She felt around in the grass. Only the empty jar. The fireflies drifted up and over the fence. Leo stood still. His lip trembled. "I'm sorry," he whispered. Maya wanted to cry. All that light, gone. She sat back on her heels and looked up.

***

Wait. The night was not empty. Above them, the moon hung round and white. Stars spread across the sky like spilled sugar. Leo pointed up. "Look," he said. "There's more light than we thought." Maya stared. The moon was so bright she could see Leo's face clearly. The stars did not blink. They stayed. "We don't need the jar," Leo said. "We have the whole sky." Maya felt something loosen in her chest. "You're right," she said. They lay back on the grass. The ground was warm. Crickets kept singing. "That one's the brightest," Leo said. "That's the North Star," Maya said. "It helps people find their way home." Leo was quiet for a moment. "We're already home," he said. Maya smiled. "Yeah," she said. "We are." They stayed there a long while. The moon moved a little. The stars stayed put. Maya did not feel scared anymore. The dark was not empty. It was full.

Maya sat up and hugged her knees. The jar lay on its side, empty. She had wanted to give Leo a light he could hold. Now there was nothing to hold.

"I'm sorry," she said. "I ruined it."

Leo sat up too. He rubbed his eyes with one hand. "You didn't ruin anything," he said. "The fireflies are still here. They're just not in the jar."

Maya looked around. He was right. Tiny lights blinked in the bushes. They were not lost. They were free.

"But you were scared," she said.

"I was," Leo said. "Then I looked up." He pointed. "There's more light than we thought."

Maya tipped her head back. The moon was a big white plate. Stars covered the sky like salt spilled on a dark table. She had seen them before, but she had never really looked.

"That one's the brightest," Leo said.

"That's the North Star," Maya said. "It helps people find their way home."

Leo was quiet. Then he said, "We're already home."

Maya smiled. "Yeah," she said. "We are."

They lay back on the grass. The ground was still warm from the day. Crickets sang in the hedge. A soft wind moved the leaves.

Maya felt something loosen in her chest. The dark was not empty. It was full of quiet things.

"I'm not scared anymore," Leo said.

"Me neither," Maya said. And it was true.

***

After a while, Leo yawned. "I'm sleepy," he said.

Maya stood up and brushed grass off her knees. "Let's go in," she said. "But we need a night light."

They went inside. The house was still dark. Maya found a small candle in the kitchen drawer. She put it in the empty jar. Then she lit it with a match.

A tiny flame jumped up. It was small, but it was enough.

"There," Maya said. "A jar of light."

Leo carried it upstairs. The flame made shadows dance on the walls. In his room, Maya set the jar on the dresser.

"It's like a little star," Leo said.

"A star we can keep," Maya said.

Leo climbed into bed. Maya pulled the blanket up to his chin. The candle glow made his face soft and gold.

"Maya?" he said.

"Yeah?"

"The dark isn't scary anymore."

Maya sat on the edge of his bed. "Why not?"

"Because you're here," Leo said. "And the light is here. And the moon is still outside."

Maya looked out the window. The moon was there, round and calm. She felt warm all over.

"Goodnight, Leo," she said.

"Goodnight, Maya," he said.

His eyes closed. His breathing got slow and even. Maya watched him for a minute. Then she blew out the candle and went to her own room. The dark did not feel empty. It felt like a soft blanket. And she was not afraid.
