Juno found the hopscotch line scuffed out again. The chalk squares on the playground were smudged into gray streaks, as if someone had dragged a wet cloth across them. She crouched and touched the concrete. Still damp.

"Third time this week," she said. "Somebody's wiping it out."

Her friend Sam jogged over, breath puffing in the cold morning air. "Maybe it's the rain."

"Rain doesn't leave streaks like that." Juno stood and brushed chalk dust from her knees. "We need a plan."

By lunch, the whole crew had gathered behind the climbing frame: Sam, who noticed everything; Ravi, who could draw anything; and Bea, who never lost a game of hide and seek. Juno laid out the facts on a scrap of paper.

"Chalk hopscotch, drawn every Monday. Erased by Tuesday morning. Three weeks running."

"Who'd bother?" Ravi asked.

"That's what we're finding out." Juno tapped the paper. "Tonight we set a trap."

The plan was simple. Ravi drew a fresh hopscotch grid that afternoon, pressing the chalk hard into the concrete. Then they sprinkled a thin line of flour along the edge of the squares. Anyone stepping close would leave a mark.

"And we take turns watching," Juno said. "From the bushes by the fence. Sam first, then me, then Bea."

"What about me?" Ravi asked.

"You're the artist. Keep drawing grids until we catch them."

That night, Sam hid behind the hedge with a flashlight turned off. He saw nothing until the moon rose, and then only a fox. When Juno took over at ten, the playground was still and silver. Bea's shift passed without a sound.

But in the morning, the hopscotch was gone again. The flour line was broken in one spot. And in the middle of the concrete, pressed into the dust, was a small footprint. It was tiny, no bigger than Juno's palm.

"That's a little kid's boot," Bea whispered. "Look at the treads."

Juno stared at the print. Her heart did a funny flip. "Somebody's been sneaking out here every night to draw, and somebody else keeps wiping it away."

Sam frowned. "So who's the culprit?"

"Both of them," Juno said. "We just have to figure out which is which."

The caretaker's shed sat at the edge of the playground, behind the big oak. Its door was green and peeling, and a broom leaned against the wall like a tired soldier. Juno knocked. Nobody answered, but the door swung open a crack.

Inside, Mr. Alvarez was pouring a bucket of soapy water into a drain. A mop stood in the corner, still dripping. He looked up and sighed. "If you're here about the graffiti, I've already cleaned it. Third time this week."

"Graffiti?" Bea said. "You mean the hopscotch?"

Mr. Alvarez nodded. "Kids shouldn't be drawing on the concrete. I wash it off every morning before school. Keeps the place tidy."

Juno stepped forward. "But it keeps coming back. Someone redraws it every night."

The caretaker frowned. "Then someone's wasting my soap." He lifted a small brush from a hook. "I found this by the fence. Little thing. Probably dropped it."

It was a stub of pink chalk, worn down to a nub. Juno turned it over. On the side, in tiny letters, someone had written a name: LILY.

***

Back at the hopscotch grid, Juno knelt and studied the footprint. The treads were worn smooth in places, like the boots had been passed down. "This isn't a vandal," she said. "It's a kid who really wants to draw."

Sam scratched his head. "So why does Mr. Alvarez keep washing it off?"

"Because he thinks it's a mess." Juno stood up. "But the kid keeps coming back. That means something."

Bea looked at the chalk stub. "Lily. Do we know a Lily?"

Ravi snapped his fingers. "The new girl! She moved into the blue house on Maple Court last month. I saw her carrying a box of chalk."

Juno nodded slowly. "She's drawing the same hopscotch every night. Why?"

"Maybe she likes hopscotch," Sam said.

"No." Juno traced the numbers with her finger. "Look at the pattern. It's not a normal grid. It's the same one from the old playground on Willow Street. My cousin showed me a photo once. Lily must have lived there before."

Bea's eyes went wide. "She's drawing her old home."

The four friends stood in a circle. "We can't just tell Mr. Alvarez to stop," Ravi said. "He'll think we're taking her side."

"Then we help her," Juno said. "But first we have to talk to her."

The blue house on Maple Court had a bicycle on its side and a box of chalk on the step. Juno knocked. A girl with braids opened the door. She looked at the four of them and then at the chalk in Juno's hand.

"You found me," Lily said quietly. "I thought I was sneaky."

"You were," Sam said. "Mostly."

Lily let them sit on the porch. She held a piece of pink chalk and turned it over and over. "The hopscotch at my old school had a sun in the middle," she said. "I drew it every day at recess. The numbers went all the way to twelve."

"Willow Street," Juno said. "Right?"

Lily nodded. "My best friend Maya and I made it up. When we moved here, I couldn't find anything like it. So I drew it at night. When nobody was looking." She looked down. "It made me feel like I was still there."

"But Mr. Alvarez washes it off," Bea said.

"I know," Lily said. "I saw him once. I hid behind the fence. He said it was a mess. But I couldn't stop." She tucked the chalk into her pocket. "I miss my old street. I miss Maya. The hopscotch is the only thing that feels the same."

Juno sat down next to her. "We can fix this. But you have to come with us."

Lily's eyes went wide. "To the caretaker? He'll be mad."

"Maybe not," Ravi said. "He's not a monster. He just doesn't know."

***

Mr. Alvarez was in his shed, sorting a bucket of soapy rags. He looked up when the five of them crowded in. "What's this?" he said. "A parade?"

Juno spoke first. "Mr. Alvarez, we know who draws the hopscotch." She stepped aside. Lily came forward, holding her chalk box.

"It's me," Lily said. "I'm sorry. I didn't mean to make a mess."

Mr. Alvarez put down the rag. "You drew that? Every night?"

Lily nodded. "It's the hopscotch from my old school. I missed it so much. I wanted to keep it."

The caretaker was quiet for a moment. "I thought it was kids being careless," he said. "But you were trying to remember something."

"I can stop," Lily said quickly.

"No," Juno said. "We have a better idea." She looked at Mr. Alvarez. "What if we gave her one square of pavement? A proper place, with permission. She could draw the hopscotch there and nobody would wash it off."

Mr. Alvarez rubbed his chin. "One square?"

"By the wall," Bea said. "Where the rain doesn't reach."

He looked at Lily. "You'd keep it neat?"

"I promise," Lily said.

Mr. Alvarez sighed. "Then I'll find some paint. Something that won't wash away. We'll do it Saturday."

Lily's face broke into a smile. "Really?"

"Really," he said. "And maybe you can teach me the rules. I was never good at hopscotch."

Sam laughed. "We'll all play."

That Saturday, the square by the wall bloomed with a bright sun and numbers up to twelve. Lily drew it herself, and Mr. Alvarez painted the outline. When she finished, she stood back and smiled.

"It's not Willow Street," she said. "But it's close."

"It's Maple Court," Juno said. "That's just as good."

Lily picked up the pink chalk and drew one more square. "Your turn," she said. "I'll teach you."

And for the first time in weeks, the hopscotch stayed.
