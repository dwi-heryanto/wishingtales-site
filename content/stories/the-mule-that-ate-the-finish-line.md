The morning of the Maple Hollow pancake race smelled of syrup and hot tar. Joss was holding one end of the finish line ribbon while Lila held the other. It was red, wide as a hand, and it said FINISH in letters made of little gold stars.

"Hold it tight," said Joss. "Mrs. Pruitt wants it straight as a ruler."

Lila pulled her end. The ribbon trembled between them, bright as a birthday. Down the lane, the pancake griddles were already smoking. Someone was tuning a tuba. Somewhere a dog barked at a balloon.

Then Biscuit wandered over.

Biscuit was their mule, gray as a rain cloud, with ears like two shovels and a look in his eye that meant trouble. He sniffed the ribbon. He sniffed it again, deeply, like a man reading a menu.

"Biscuit," said Lila slowly. "No."

Biscuit took the ribbon in his teeth the way other mules take hay. There was a soft, cheerful crunching sound. The FINISH letters disappeared one by one, star by star.

Joss stared at his empty hands. "He ate it."

"He ate all of it," said Lila.

Biscuit licked his lips and looked pleased with himself. A small gold star was stuck to his chin like a crumb. The griddles hissed. The tuba went oompah. Somebody's grandmother shouted that the pancakes were ready.

And the first runner, a tall man in a yellow shirt, was already stretching his legs at the far end of the lane.

***

Mrs. Pruitt arrived at a trot, clipboard first. She was small and quick and wore a whistle around her neck even when she slept, or so people said. She looked at the ribbon, or where the ribbon had been, and her whistle gave a little squeak of its own.

"Where," she said, "is my finish line?"

"Biscuit ate it," said Joss.

"He didn't mean anything by it," said Lila. "He's very sorry. Look at his face."

Biscuit's face showed no sorrow at all. It showed interest in the clipboard.

Mrs. Pruitt pressed a hand to her forehead. "Thirty years I have run this race. Thirty years, and never once has a mule eaten my finish line. The runners are coming. The mayor is coming. I shall have to cancel."

"You can't cancel," said Lila. "Everyone's here. Everyone's hungry."

"I have no finish line," said Mrs. Pruitt, "and no time to get one. The race starts in four minutes."

Joss looked down the lane. The yellow-shirted man was jogging on the spot. Behind him, a whole crowd of runners was gathering like a tide. His stomach did a small, unpleasant flip.

"We'll fix it," he said.

"Before the first runner gets here," said Lila.

Mrs. Pruitt narrowed her eyes at them, then at Biscuit. "Four minutes," she said. "Not one second more. I am going to go and lie down near the pancake tent."

She marched off. Biscuit chewed the corner of a poster as if nothing had happened at all.

Joss ran to the supply tent and came back with a coil of rope. "This'll do it," he puffed, looping it across the lane and tying one end to a fence post. "Hold that end, Lila." Lila grabbed the rope and pulled it tight. It sagged in the middle like a slack clothesline. "It's too low," she said. "Runners will trip." Joss tugged harder. The fence post wobbled. Then Biscuit ambled over, sniffed the rope, and took a thoughtful bite. "No!" shouted Joss. The rope snapped clean in two. Biscuit chewed happily. "Rope's out," said Lila. "Scarf!" She whipped off her red scarf and tied it between two posts. It fluttered nicely. Biscuit wandered up and nibbled the fringe. "Stop that!" said Lila. The scarf came loose and fell in the dust. Biscuit nosed it, disappointed. "Sausages," said Joss. "They're shiny and long and we can tie them." He grabbed a string of sausages from the pancake stall. The twins stretched it across the lane. It looked splendid. Then Biscuit took one end in his teeth and slurped the whole string up like spaghetti. "Gone," said Lila. The yellow-shirted man was jogging closer. "Two minutes!" he called. Joss felt his ears go hot. Mrs. Pruitt's voice floated from the pancake tent: "Is it fixed?" "Nearly!" Lila shouted back. She didn't sound nearly at all.

***

Lila stared at Biscuit. The mule was licking his lips and gazing at the mayor's brass band, who were tuning up near the start line. Their instruments gleamed in the sun. Biscuit's ears swivelled. "He loves shiny things," said Lila slowly. "He ate the ribbon because it shone. He ate the sausages because they were glossy. He's a magpie in a mule's body." Joss looked at the band. The mayor was adjusting his hat. A kazoo poked from his pocket. "The band has kazoos," said Joss. "Shiny ones." Lila's eyes went wide. "We borrow them. We line them up across the lane. Biscuit will follow the shine, and the runners cross behind him." "Mrs. Pruitt will never allow it," said Joss. "She's lying down," said Lila. "Quick!" They sprinted to the band leader. "Please," said Lila, "can we borrow your kazoos? It's an emergency." The band leader blinked. "All of them?" "All of them," said Joss. The band leader shrugged and handed over a fistful of kazoos. The twins raced back and laid them in a wobbly line across the lane. Biscuit trotted over, nose twitching. The first runner appeared at the bend. "Ready?" gasped Lila. Joss nodded. Biscuit lowered his head and began to sniff the shiny line.

Biscuit lowered his head and sniffed the first kazoo. Then he licked it. The kazoo let out a long, wobbly hum. Biscuit jumped back, ears flat. The runner at the bend was getting closer. "Blow one!" yelled Joss. Lila grabbed a kazoo and blew. It squeaked like a startled mouse. Biscuit's ears shot up. He trotted toward the sound, nose twitching. The first runner, a tall girl in red shorts, reached the kazoo line. She stopped and stared. "What is this?" she panted. "The finish line," said Joss. "Cross it!" The girl stepped over the kazoos and kept running. Biscuit followed her, sniffing the shiny line. The second runner arrived, then the third. Each one paused, laughed, and stepped over the kazoos. Biscuit trotted along the line, licking each kazoo in turn. Every lick made a funny hum. The crowd began to clap and cheer. Mrs. Pruitt sat up from her bench. Her mouth fell open. "What in the world?" she said. "It's the finish line," said Lila. "Biscuit is leading the parade." The mule reached the end of the kazoos and turned around. He walked back along the line, humming and snorting. The runners crossed behind him, one by one. The last runner, a small boy with a bandage on his knee, stopped to pat Biscuit. "Good mule," he said. Biscuit nuzzled his pocket. The boy laughed and pulled out a shiny wrapper. Biscuit ate it. "Biscuit!" cried Joss. But the boy just laughed harder. Mrs. Pruitt stood up and brushed off her skirt. "Well," she said. "I have never seen a finish like that. But it worked." She looked at the twins. "You two fixed it. I don't know how, but you did." The crowd cheered again. Biscuit lifted his head and brayed loudly. It sounded like a kazoo with a cold.

***

After the race, the mayor handed out medals. They were shiny and gold, and Biscuit tried to eat three of them. Joss held him back while Lila collected their own medals. "We couldn't have done it without you," said Lila. She pulled a carrot from her pocket. It was long and orange and slightly limp. "Here," she said. "Your medal." Biscuit took the carrot gently. He chewed it with his eyes half closed. Then Lila tied a string around another carrot and hung it around his neck. Biscuit wore it proudly. He strutted around the fairgrounds, showing off his carrot medal to anyone who would look. Mrs. Pruitt found them by the bandstand. "I owe you an apology," she said. "I panicked. You two kept your heads." She patted Biscuit's neck. "And this one, well, he's a menace. But a clever menace." The band leader walked over. "The kazoos were a hit," he said. "People want to know if we can do it again next year." Mrs. Pruitt looked at the twins. "What do you think?" Joss grinned. "Only if Biscuit leads the parade." Lila nodded. "And only if we get to keep the kazoos." The mayor laughed. "I'll make it official," he said. "From now on, the Maple Hollow pancake race ends with a kazoo line." Biscuit brayed again. The sound echoed across the fairgrounds. Joss and Lila looked at each other. Then they looked at their mule, standing tall with his carrot medal. "Same time next year?" said Joss. "Same time next year," said Lila. Biscuit chewed his carrot and nodded, as if he understood every word.
