Maya noticed it before anyone else did. The glass case in the music room stood open, and the little velvet cushion inside was empty. The school's violin — the old one everyone called the Redbird — was gone.

"It was here on Friday," she said. "I saw Mr. Okafor lock the case himself."

Her best friend Sam leaned in beside her. "Are you sure? Maybe they moved it."

"Nobody moves the Redbird." Maya pressed her palm flat on the cool glass. "It's older than the whole school. It's on the banner in the gym."

The music teacher came in then, still holding her coffee, and stopped dead in the doorway. "Where is it?" she whispered. "Where's the violin?"

No one knew. The case wasn't broken. Nothing else in the room had been touched. Maya looked at the empty cushion and felt something click into place, like a key in a lock. She had read every mystery book in the school library twice. Now she had one of her own.

"I'll find it," she said quietly.

"You'll what?" said Sam.

"Find it. I know how this works." Maya pulled a small notebook from her back pocket and tapped it against her hand. "We start with questions and we start today."

They began in the hallway, where a poster of the Redbird hung crooked. Maya straightened it and copied down every word. "Made in 1892," she read. "Donated by the Harrow family." She underlined the name twice.

Sam followed her, chewing his lip. "Maya, what if it was stolen? What if someone dangerous has it?"

"Then we find out who." She smiled, a little nervous and a little thrilled. "And we find out why."

***

After school they found the janitor, Mr. Bell, mopping the long hallway by the music room. Maya showed him her notebook.

"Were you here on Friday night?" she asked.

"Every night," said Mr. Bell. "I lock up at nine."

"Did you see anything strange?"

He rested his mop against the wall and thought hard. "A window was open in the music room. Just a crack. I shut it and thought nothing of it." He frowned. "Now I wonder."

Sam was already at the window, crouched on the sill. "Maya. Come look."

A little ribbon was caught on the latch. It was red and thin, the kind you tie in a girl's hair, and it had been torn clean through. Maya held it up to the light.

"Someone left in a hurry," she said.

"Or someone was dragged," Sam whispered, and then shook his head. "No. No, that's a ribbon."

Mr. Bell leaned over to look. "That's odd. Not many people wear those anymore." He picked up his mop. "You two be careful now. No sneaking about at night."

"Of course not," Maya said, and she crossed her fingers behind her back.

Outside, the sky was turning orange. Sam turned the ribbon over and over in his fingers. "So what now?"

Maya looked back at the darkened music room, her jaw set. "Now we watch the window." She opened her notebook to a fresh page. "Tonight."

The school at night was a different building. Hallways stretched long and dark. Every little sound bounced twice as far. Maya and Sam crouched behind the hedge by the music room window, keeping very still.

"My knees hurt," Sam whispered.

"Shh. Knees are the least of it." Maya watched the window, her notebook open on her lap. "If someone comes back for the violin, they'll come here."

They waited. An hour went by. Maya counted cars on the road, then stars, then her own heartbeats. Sam's head started to droop.

Then a shadow moved along the side of the building.

It was small and quick, hood up, and it went straight to the window. The latch clicked. Maya grabbed Sam's arm. Together they crept around the hedge, keeping low.

"Hey!" Maya called out. "Stop right there!"

The figure spun. For one second Maya saw a pale face under the hood, wide eyes, a wisp of hair. Then the stranger bolted across the yard and over the low wall, and was gone into the dark.

Sam ran to the wall and peered over. "Nothing. Just a street and some bushes."

Maya knelt by the window. Something had fallen in the scramble, half under a stone. She picked it up. A key, small and brass, with a paper tag tied to it. On the tag, in careful pen, was a word.

"What does it say?" Sam asked.

Maya turned it toward the streetlight. "Larkin's." She looked up at him. "That's the music shop on Bridge Street."

***

Larkin's Music sat between a bakery and a shoe shop, its window full of dusty guitars. Maya pushed the door open and a little bell rang above her head. Inside smelled of wood and rosin and old paper.

Behind the counter stood a tall old man with grey whiskers. "Help you two?"

Maya set the brass key on the counter. "Do you know who this belongs to?"

The man picked it up and turned it over. His face changed, just a little. "Where did you find this?"

"Near our school," Sam said. "Someone dropped it."

"Hm." The man set the key down carefully. "That key opens the back door of my shop. I gave it to my granddaughter. She helps me here after school."

Maya's heart thumped. "Does she wear red ribbons?"

The man went quiet. Then he sighed and reached under the counter. "I think," he said, "there's something you should know about that violin." He pulled out a soft cloth and laid it flat. "Your school's violin has a crack in the belly. Small, but it will only get worse. I've been mending instruments for forty years, and I told your music teacher I'd fix it quiet, before the spring concert, as a surprise."

Sam blinked. "So it wasn't stolen?"

"Not stolen." The old man's eyes crinkled. "But my granddaughter took it without asking, and that was wrong. She thought she was helping me." He looked at Maya. "She's been frightened to bring it back."

Maya thought about the shadow running into the dark. "Where is she now?"

"In the back," said Mr. Larkin. "Waiting."

Maya pushed through the curtain into the back room. It smelled of wood shavings and varnish. A girl about her age sat on a stool, hugging a black case to her chest. Red ribbons hung from her braids, frayed at the ends. "You're the one who took it," Maya said, not a question.

The girl looked up. Her eyes were red. "I'm sorry," she whispered. "I didn't steal it. I was fixing it."

"Fixing it?" Sam stepped in behind Maya. "You're a kid."

"I help my grandpa every day after school." The girl held out the case. "I know how to do small repairs. The crack was getting worse. I heard the music teacher say they couldn't afford to fix it before the concert."

Maya stared at the case. "So you took it without asking."

"I thought I could bring it back good as new. But the glue needed to dry. Then I got scared." The girl's voice wobbled. "I'm Lily."

Mr. Larkin came in and put a hand on Lily's shoulder. "She told me tonight. I said we had to bring it back first thing tomorrow. With an apology."

Maya looked at the case. Then at Lily's frightened face. "Can I see it?"

Lily opened the case. The violin lay inside, its wooden belly smooth and whole. A thin line showed where the crack had been, sealed neat as a scar. "It's fixed," Maya said.

"It's not perfect," Lily said. "But it will hold."

Sam crouched down. "You should have asked. But you did a good job."

Lily's chin trembled. "Are you going to tell on me?"

Maya thought about the dark shape running away. It hadn't been a thief. It had been a girl, scared and trying to help. "We're going to bring it back together," she said. "And you're going to tell the truth."

***

The next morning, Maya and Sam walked into the music room with Lily between them. The violin case felt warm in Maya's hands. Ms. Chen was at her desk, marking papers.

"Maya? You're early."

"We found the violin," Maya said. She set the case on the desk and opened it. "It wasn't stolen. It was being repaired."

Ms. Chen stood up. She looked at the violin, then at Lily. "Repaired? By whom?"

"By me," Lily said. Her voice was small but steady. "I'm sorry I took it without asking. I help at my grandpa's shop. I thought I could fix the crack before the concert."

Ms. Chen picked up the violin and turned it in the light. She ran a finger along the sealed crack. "This is good work," she said quietly. "Very good."

"I should have asked," Lily said. "I know that now."

Ms. Chen set the violin down. "Yes. You should have." She looked at Maya. "And you? You found the truth."

Maya nodded. "It took all night."

A smile tugged at Ms. Chen's mouth. "Then perhaps you'd like to help Lily explain to the whole orchestra why the violin was gone. And Lily, you can tell them how you fixed it."

Lily's eyes went wide. "Really?"

"Really. After you apologize."

That afternoon, the orchestra gathered around. Lily told them everything. When she finished, the room was quiet. Then the first violinist clapped. Soon everyone was clapping.

Maya stood at the back with Sam. The violin sang out as Ms. Chen tuned it. "You know," Sam whispered, "I thought we were chasing a thief."

"So did I," Maya said. "But some mysteries have kinder answers than you expect."

She looked at Lily, who was smiling now, red ribbons bright against her braids. The music filled the room, and Maya felt something settle in her chest. She had wanted to solve the case. What she found was better.
