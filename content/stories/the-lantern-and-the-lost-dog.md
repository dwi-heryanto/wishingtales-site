The paper plates were still warm from the grill when Scrap slipped through the gap in the fence.

Milo saw the whole thing. One second his dog was begging for a dropped hot dog. The next, a grey tail vanished between the slats.

"Scrap!" Milo shouted. He ran to the fence and pressed his face against the wood. Nothing but dark grass and the hum of crickets.

"He'll come back," said his uncle, waving a spatula. "Dogs always come back."

Milo nodded, but his stomach felt like a knot. The backyard party went on around him. Grown-ups laughed. Someone turned up the radio. Milo stood by the fence with his hands in his pockets.

He counted to sixty. Then he counted again. No Scrap.

The sky turned the colour of old pennies. The first star blinked on. Milo's mom called him to the table, and he ate a burger without tasting it.

"You're quiet," she said.

"I'm fine," said Milo.

But he wasn't fine. Scrap was small and fast, and the neighbourhood was big and strange after dark. Every minute that passed made the knot in his stomach pull tighter.

He slipped away from the table and stood at the gate. The street stretched off into shadow. A dog barked somewhere far off, and Milo's heart jumped. Then it went quiet again.

He was just one kid standing in a driveway. He had never gone looking for anything in the dark before.

***

A bike bell rang behind him. Milo turned. Priya rolled up to the gate, her front basket rattling.

"I heard about Scrap," she said. She reached into the basket and lifted out a glass jar. Inside, a candle glowed, and the light bounced around the glass like a tiny moon.

"My grandpa makes these," she said. "For power cuts. I brought it for us."

Milo stared at the jar. "Us?"

"Us," said Priya. "You're not going alone." She propped her bike against the fence. "Which way did he go?"

Milo pointed down the street. His hand shook a little. "That way. Past the Johnsons'."

"Then that's the way we go." Priya held the jar up. The light made a warm circle on the pavement.

Milo looked back at his house. The windows were bright and loud. Then he looked at Priya, standing there with her lantern and her chin up.

The knot in his stomach loosened just a little.

"Okay," he said.

"Okay," she said. "Stay close. Two sets of eyes are better than one."

They started walking. The jar swung gently between them, and the dark leaned in around the light. Milo could hear his own steps on the pavement. He could hear Priya breathing beside him.

"We'll find him," she said.

Milo wanted to believe her. He took one more step, and then another, into the dark street.

The street was quiet. Most houses had their lights off, and the dark felt thick between them. Priya held the jar lantern high. Its glow caught the edge of a hedge, a mailbox, a garden gnome.

"There," Milo said. He pointed at something small and round on the sidewalk.

It was Scrap's ball. The red one he chewed all day. It was wet with spit and dirt.

"That's his," Milo said. "He never drops it."

Priya crouched down. The lantern light made her shadow long. "So he came this way."

Milo picked up the ball. It was still warm from the day. He put it in his pocket.

They walked on. The houses grew farther apart. A cat watched them from a porch and didn't move.

"What's that?" Priya said.

She knelt by a low wall. A muddy paw print sat on the stone. It was fresh.

"Four toes," Milo said. "That's Scrap."

"Then he's close."

They followed the prints. They crossed a small park. The grass was cold and wet on Milo's sneakers. He didn't mind. He walked faster now. The dark didn't feel so big.

"You're not shaking anymore," Priya said.

Milo looked at his hand. She was right.

***

A gate banged somewhere behind them.

Milo jumped. His heart hit his ribs. "What was that?"

"Just a gate," Priya said. "The wind."

"The wind doesn't open gates."

"It does when they're loose."

They stood still. The street was black ahead of them. No streetlights. No cars. Just the small circle of jar light and the two of them.

Milo's throat went tight. "Maybe we should go back." He heard his own voice and didn't like it. "Maybe he went home while we were out."

Priya didn't move. "He didn't."

"You don't know that."

"I know he's your dog," she said. "And I know you were brave enough to start. That's the hard part. This is just the rest."

Milo looked at the dark street. It looked back at him. He thought of Scrap's ball in his pocket. He thought of Scrap alone somewhere, waiting.

"I can't hear him," Milo said. "What if he's hurt?"

"Then we find him faster," Priya said. "Come on."

She took his hand. Her hand was small and warm. The lantern swung between them.

Milo took a breath. Then he walked. One step. Then another.

The dark leaned in, but it didn't stop them.

Milo saw the fence first. It was a low wooden fence at the end of the alley, with a gap at the bottom. Something moved behind it. A small, scruffy shape. "Scrap!" Milo called, and his voice cracked. The shape whined. It was Scrap, all right. His tail gave a weak wag. He was stuck between the fence and a bush, his collar caught on a nail. "Oh no," Priya said. "He's tangled." Milo knelt down. The lantern light showed Scrap's scared eyes. "It's okay, boy," Milo said. "We're here." Scrap licked his hand. His tongue was dry. "We need to get him out," Priya said. "Can you lift the fence?" Milo tried. It was too heavy. "It won't budge." "Then we'll have to push him back," Priya said. "No, he's stuck." Milo felt the panic rise again. "What if we hurt him?" "We won't," Priya said. "We'll be gentle. You talk to him. I'll work the collar." Milo nodded. He put his face close to Scrap's. "Remember the ball?" he whispered. "We'll play tomorrow. I promise." Scrap's ears perked. Priya reached in slowly. Her fingers found the buckle. "Got it," she said. The collar popped open. Scrap scrambled backward, then forward, into Milo's arms. He was muddy and shaking. Milo held him tight. "You're okay," Milo said. "You're okay."

***

Back home, the barbecue was over. The yard was dark except for the porch light. Milo's mom ran out when she saw them. "Scrap! Oh, thank goodness." She hugged Milo and Priya both. "You two went out there alone?" "We had to," Milo said. His voice was steady now. His mom looked at him for a long moment. "I'm proud of you," she said. "Both of you. But next time, wake me." "We will," Priya said. Scrap drank a whole bowl of water. Then he curled up on Milo's feet and fell asleep. Priya's mom came to get her. "Thank you," Milo said. "For the lantern. For everything." "You did the hard part," Priya said. "I just held the light." She smiled and left. Milo sat on the porch step. The night was quiet now. The stars were out. He thought about the dark street and how big it had seemed. It didn't seem big anymore. Scrap snored softly. Milo touched the ball in his pocket. He had been scared. But he had gone anyway. That was the thing. He hadn't known he could do that. Now he did. His mom sat beside him. "Bedtime," she said. "I know," Milo said. But he didn't move yet. He wanted to keep this moment a little longer. The night felt like a gift. Not scary. Just wide and full of stars. He picked up Scrap, who was heavier than he remembered. "Come on, boy," he said. "Let's go home."
